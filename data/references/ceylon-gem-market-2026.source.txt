Blue Sapphire Price in Sri Lanka
Blue sapphire is one of the best-known gemstones associated with Sri Lanka, historically known as Ceylon. However, there is no fixed price for a blue sapphire. Two stones with the same carat weight can have very different values because price depends on a combination of color, clarity, cut, shape, carat weight, treatment, certification, origin and overall quality.

As a broad retail-market guide, natural blue sapphires may range from approximately US$300 to US$6,000+ per carat, while exceptional larger, unheated or collector-quality stones can sell substantially above this range.

Natural blue sapphire from Sri Lanka
What Affects Blue Sapphire Price?
Color is one of the most important value factors. Attractive blue sapphires usually show good saturation and brightness without appearing too pale, grayish or excessively dark. Vivid and evenly distributed blue color can command a substantial premium.

Clarity also affects value. Natural sapphires commonly contain inclusions, but transparent and eye-clean stones generally command higher prices. Heavy inclusions, fractures, cloudiness or reduced transparency can lower value. However, clarity should always be judged together with color and overall appearance.

Cut determines how effectively the stone displays its color and brilliance. A well-cut sapphire should have balanced proportions, good symmetry and minimal windowing or excessive dark areas. Poor cutting can make an otherwise attractive stone appear dull or washed out.

Shape can also influence demand and cutting yield. Oval and cushion shapes are common because they can retain more weight from sapphire rough. Round sapphires may sometimes command a premium when well cut because more rough may be sacrificed during cutting. Pear, emerald, heart, marquise and other shapes can also be valuable when their proportions, symmetry and overall quality are strong.

Shape alone does not determine value. An exceptional oval sapphire can be worth significantly more than an average round sapphire if its color, clarity, treatment status and overall quality are better.

Blue Sapphire Price by Carat Weight
Carat weight can strongly affect value, particularly when desirable quality is maintained as the sapphire becomes larger.

Once a fine blue sapphire reaches approximately 10 carats or more, a general price-per-carat range becomes much less reliable because stones of this size are considerably rarer and quality differences can create very large differences in value.

For sapphires around 20 ct, 50 ct or 100 ct+, price cannot be estimated reliably from carat weight alone. Large exceptional sapphires should normally be evaluated individually according to their color, clarity, cut, treatment, laboratory reports, origin, rarity, provenance and market demand.

For this reason, there is no practical fixed maximum price for an exceptional large blue sapphire.

Heated vs Unheated Blue Sapphire Price
Heat treatment is common in the sapphire market and is generally used to improve color or clarity. A good-quality heated sapphire can still have significant value.

However, when two sapphires have similar color, clarity, cut and carat weight, a fine unheated sapphire may command a higher price because high-quality untreated material is rarer.

The price difference is not fixed. An unheated sapphire with weak color and poor clarity does not automatically become more valuable than a beautiful, well-cut heated sapphire.

Treatment should therefore be considered together with the stone’s overall quality, rather than used as the only measure of value.

Certification and Blue Sapphire Value
A laboratory certificate does not automatically make a sapphire valuable, but it can increase buyer confidence by independently confirming important information such as whether the stone is natural and whether detectable treatments are present.

For valuable blue sapphires—particularly larger or unheated stones—a report from a respected gemological laboratory can be especially useful.

Certification can support marketability, but the sapphire’s actual color, clarity, cut, size and overall appearance remain fundamental value factors.

Why Can Blue Sapphire Prices Exceed These Ranges?
Some blue sapphires can sell substantially above the rough ranges shown in this guide when several desirable characteristics occur together, including:

exceptional, vivid blue color
high transparency and clarity
excellent cutting and proportions
large carat weight
confirmed unheated status
reliable laboratory certification
rare or highly regarded origin
exceptional rarity or provenance
strong collector or market demand
A commercial-quality 5 ct sapphire and an exceptional 5 ct unheated sapphire should therefore not be expected to have the same price per carat.

Yellow Sapphire Price in Sri Lanka
Yellow sapphire is one of the most popular colored sapphires found in Sri Lanka. Prices can vary considerably depending on color, clarity, cut, shape, carat weight, treatment and certification. As a rough market guide, natural yellow sapphire may sell from around US$30 to US$1,200+ per carat, while fine unheated, larger or exceptional stones can exceed this range.

There is no fixed price per carat for yellow sapphire. Two stones of the same weight can have very different values because the overall quality of the gemstone is more important than weight alone.

Natural yellow sapphire from Sri Lanka
What Affects Yellow Sapphire Price?
Color is one of the main pricing factors. Bright and attractive yellow, golden-yellow or slightly orangy-yellow stones generally command stronger prices than very pale or dull stones. Strong saturation and even color distribution can significantly increase value.

Clarity is also important. Transparent and eye-clean yellow sapphires usually command higher prices, while visible inclusions, fractures or cloudy areas can reduce market value. However, color and overall appearance should always be considered together with clarity.

Cut affects brightness and how well the stone displays its color. A well-cut yellow sapphire with good proportions and minimal windowing generally has stronger market appeal than a poorly cut stone.

Shape can influence buyer demand and cutting yield. Oval, cushion, round and pear shapes are common, but shape alone does not determine price. An exceptional oval sapphire can be worth more than an average round sapphire if its color and clarity are superior.

Yellow Sapphire Price by Carat Weight
As carat weight increases, fine-quality yellow sapphires can become more valuable per carat, especially when good color and clarity are maintained.

A smaller commercial-quality stone may fall near the lower end of the market, while a larger vivid, transparent and unheated sapphire may command a much higher price.

Heated vs Unheated Yellow Sapphire
Heat treatment is common in sapphire and can improve color or clarity. A heated yellow sapphire can still have good value, but a fine unheated yellow sapphire may command a premium when its natural color and clarity are attractive.

Certification can also increase buyer confidence, particularly for larger or unheated stones.

Important: Yellow sapphire prices are only approximate market guides. Actual values may be lower or substantially higher depending on color, clarity, cut, carat weight, treatment, certification, origin, rarity and market demand.

Pink Sapphire Price in Sri Lanka
Pink sapphire prices in Sri Lanka vary widely, but as a rough market guide they can range from around US$400 to US$6,000+ per carat. Fine unheated stones, vivid colors, larger sizes and exceptional clarity can sell well above general market ranges.

Current retail examples of Ceylon pink sapphires show how wide the market can be: recent listings around 1–4 carats range from roughly US$800 to more than US$3,300 per carat, depending on color intensity, clarity, size and treatment.

Natural pink sapphire from Sri Lanka
Which Pink Sapphire Colors Are More Valuable?
Pink sapphire can range from light pink through stronger pink and slightly purplish pink, with weak to intense saturation. GIA notes that pink sapphires occupy color ranges outside those used for ruby or purple sapphire.

In general:

very pale pink usually falls toward the lower end of the market
medium pink with attractive brightness can command stronger prices
vivid, evenly distributed pink can receive a substantial premium
purplish-pink stones can also be desirable when the color remains lively rather than dark or grayish
uneven color zoning can reduce value
Color should always be judged together with transparency and overall appearance.

Pink Sapphire Price by Size
Size matters, but there is no simple fixed progression.

A fine 2 ct pink sapphire can command a much higher price per carat than an average 4 ct stone if the smaller stone has better color, clarity and treatment status.

Recent Ceylon examples illustrate this variation: a 2.54 ct vivid heated pink sapphire was listed at about US$3,357/ct, while a 4.01 ct light unheated stone was listed around US$2,035/ct.

This is why carat weight alone should never be used to determine pink sapphire value.

Pink Sapphire, Ruby and Padparadscha
Pink sapphire belongs to the same mineral species, corundum, as ruby. The boundary between ruby and pink sapphire can vary in trade usage. GIA notes that in Sri Lanka some pink corundum has historically been considered ruby, while GIA requires red to be the dominant hue for ruby classification.

Padparadscha is different again. It refers to a rare sapphire color generally described as light to medium pinkish orange to orange-pink, not ordinary pink sapphire.

Because these color classifications can affect rarity and buyer perception, laboratory identification can be especially useful for high-value stones.

Unheated Pink Sapphire Premium
Heating is used in the sapphire trade and can modify color. GIA has specifically documented low-temperature treatment of pink sapphires, including material from Ratnapura, Sri Lanka.

When two stones have comparable color, clarity and cut, a fine unheated pink sapphire may command a premium because untreated material is rarer. GIA notes that confirmation of no heat can add rarity and value to fine sapphire.

Important: Pink sapphire does not have a fixed price per carat. Actual prices can vary substantially with color, clarity, size, cut, treatment, certification, rarity and current market demand.

Purple Sapphire Price in Sri Lanka
Natural purple sapphire from Sri Lanka can range from soft lavender and violet tones to deeper reddish-purple shades. As a rough market guide, prices may start around US$100 per carat and can rise to US$4,000+ per carat for finer stones.

The biggest influence is usually the quality of the purple color. Pale or grayish stones generally fall toward the lower end, while brighter, richer purple with good saturation and even color distribution tends to attract stronger prices. Stones that appear overly dark can lose brightness and may be less desirable.

Natural purple sapphire from Sri Lanka
Purple sapphire is especially interesting because slight changes in hue can produce noticeably different appearances. Some stones lean toward violet, while others show a warmer reddish-purple. Neither direction automatically makes a stone more valuable—the important factors are attractiveness, saturation, brightness and how evenly the color appears across the stone.

Clarity and transparency also matter. A transparent stone with few distracting inclusions generally commands more than a cloudy or heavily included sapphire. Cutting should enhance the color rather than create a large window or dark areas.

Larger purple sapphires can command strong prices when good color and transparency are maintained, but carat weight alone does not determine value. A smaller vivid stone may sell for more per carat than a larger dull stone.

Heated and unheated purple sapphires are both available. Fine unheated stones can receive a premium when their natural color and overall quality are attractive.

Price note: The US$100–$4,000+ per carat range is only broad market guidance. Exceptional stones may exceed it depending on color, clarity, size, treatment, certification and rarity.

Orange Sapphire Price in Sri Lanka
Orange sapphire is a natural fancy-color sapphire that can occur in Sri Lanka, but fine unheated orange sapphire is relatively uncommon. Many orange sapphires seen in the market have undergone heat treatment, and some stones may also have had their color modified by diffusion treatment.

As a rough market guide, orange sapphire may range from around US$100 to US$4,000+ per carat, but treatment status can make a major difference to value. Fine natural unheated stones with attractive orange color, good transparency and strong overall quality can sell above general market ranges.

Natural orange sapphire from Sri Lanka
The most valuable orange sapphires usually show an attractive, lively orange color with good brightness. Pale, brownish or unevenly colored stones generally trade at lower levels, while richer and more evenly distributed orange can command stronger prices.

It is also important not to confuse orange sapphire with padparadscha sapphire. Padparadscha is a much narrower pink-orange to orange-pink sapphire category. A sapphire can have beautiful orange color and still correctly be classified simply as orange sapphire.

Treatment Has a Major Effect on Orange Sapphire Price
Orange sapphire should ideally be considered in three treatment groups:

Natural unheated orange sapphire – rarer and may command a strong premium when quality is high
Heat-treated orange sapphire – still a natural sapphire and commonly accepted in the market
Diffusion-treated orange sapphire – color has been altered using additional treatment and generally trades differently from untreated or heat-only material
For higher-value orange sapphire, laboratory testing is especially useful because visual inspection alone cannot always reliably determine treatment history.

Clarity, cutting and carat weight also influence value. A transparent, well-cut stone with good color can be worth considerably more than a larger stone with weak color, heavy inclusions or poor proportions.

Important: Orange sapphire has no fixed price per carat. The US$100–$4,000+ range is only rough market guidance, and actual value depends strongly on color, clarity, cut, size, treatment, certification, rarity and market demand.

Padparadscha Sapphire Price in Sri Lanka
Padparadscha sapphire is one of the rarest and most distinctive varieties of sapphire associated with Sri Lanka. As a rough market guide, natural padparadscha sapphire may range from around US$1,000 to US$5,000+ per carat, while exceptional unheated, certified or larger stones can sell substantially above this range.

There is no fixed price per carat because the value depends heavily on whether the stone shows the attractive balance of pink and orange expected from padparadscha, together with clarity, size, cut, treatment and laboratory opinion.

Why Is Padparadscha Sapphire Expensive?
Rarity is the main reason. Fine stones showing an attractive combination of pink and orange are much less common than ordinary yellow, blue or pink sapphires.

A high-value padparadscha generally benefits from:

an appealing balance of pink and orange
good brightness rather than an overly dark appearance
relatively even color distribution
good transparency
attractive cutting
larger size while maintaining quality
unheated status
reliable laboratory documentation
Color is especially important because a stone that appears predominantly yellow, orange or pink may be classified differently rather than accepted as padparadscha.

Padparadscha Color and Price
Not every pink-orange sapphire automatically qualifies as padparadscha.

The most desirable stones generally show a delicate combination of pinkish orange to orangy pink rather than a strongly brown, yellow or red appearance.

Because the color boundary can be difficult to judge visually, especially in expensive stones, laboratory opinion can become important.

This also means two sapphires with similar weight can have very different prices if one has a highly desirable padparadscha-type color and the other does not.

Treatment and Certification
Treatment status can have a major effect on value. A fine unheated padparadscha sapphire may command a considerable premium because naturally attractive untreated stones are rare.

For higher-value stones, independent laboratory testing is strongly recommended. A laboratory report can help document whether the sapphire is natural, whether treatments are detected and whether its color characteristics meet the laboratory’s criteria for padparadscha terminology.

Important: Padparadscha sapphire prices are approximate market guides only. Exceptional color, larger size, unheated status, high clarity, rarity and respected certification can push individual stones far above ordinary market ranges.

Star Sapphire Price in Sri Lanka
Star sapphire prices in Sri Lanka can vary very widely depending on the stone’s body color, strength and sharpness of the star, transparency, size, cut, treatment and rarity. As a rough market guide, natural star sapphire may begin around US$50 per carat, while fine examples can reach US$2,000+ per carat or more.

Current Ceylon star-sapphire listings show just how broad the market can be: grey or white stones may be listed below US$100–$200/ct, medium-quality blue stones around a few hundred dollars per carat, while stronger pink examples can exceed US$2,000/ct.

Natural star sapphire from Sri Lanka showing asterism
What Makes a Star Sapphire More Valuable?
The most important factor is the asterism, or visible star effect.

A more valuable star sapphire usually has:

a sharp, well-defined star
rays that are reasonably complete and centered
a star that moves clearly across the surface when the stone is moved under light
attractive body color
good translucency without excessive cloudiness
a well-shaped cabochon dome
strong overall symmetry and polish
A weak, broken or off-center star generally reduces value, even when the stone has attractive color.

Body Color and Star Quality
Star sapphires occur in several colors, including blue, grey, pink, purple and near-colorless varieties.

Body color can make a substantial difference in price. A strong pink or attractive blue star sapphire may command much more than a grey or near-colorless stone of similar weight.

For example, current Sri Lankan listings include a 1.64 ct near-colorless star sapphire at about US$122/ct, a 12.35 ct greyish-blue example around US$337/ct, and a 3.24 ct pink star sapphire around US$2,284/ct.

Does Size Increase Star Sapphire Price?
Larger star sapphires are not automatically more valuable per carat.

A large stone with weak color or a poor star may sell for less per carat than a smaller stone with excellent color and a sharp asterism. Current Ceylon examples include a 40.62 ct blue star sapphire at about US$495/ct, showing that even very large stones can have a price per carat determined more by quality than size alone.

Treatment and Star Sapphire Value
Treatment status also matters. Natural star sapphires with no enhancement can command stronger prices when the color and star effect are attractive.

Because star sapphires rely on internal inclusions to create asterism, buyers should consider star quality, body color and treatment together, rather than judging the stone by carat weight alone.

Important: Star sapphire does not have a fixed price per carat. The US$50–$2,000+ range is only broad market guidance, and exceptional stones can sell above it.

Bi-color Sapphire Price in Sri Lanka
Bi-color sapphire, sometimes called parti-colored sapphire, displays two visibly different colors within the same stone. Sri Lankan examples can show combinations such as blue and yellow, blue and green, yellow and green, or other contrasting zones.

As a rough market guide, natural bi-color sapphire may range from around US$100 to US$4,000+ per carat. Exceptional stones with attractive contrasting colors, high transparency, good cutting and larger size may sell above this range.

Natural bi-color sapphire from Sri Lanka
When Does Color Zoning Add Value?
Normally, uneven color zoning can reduce the value of a sapphire because uniform color is generally preferred. However, bi-color or parti-colored sapphire is different: the contrasting colors are intentionally displayed as part of the stone’s character. GIA notes that parti-colored sapphires showing combinations such as blue-orange or blue-green have established demand in some markets.

A bi-color sapphire generally becomes more desirable when:

both colors are clearly visible face-up
the colors create an attractive contrast
the zoning looks intentional rather than patchy
transparency is good
the cut positions the colors attractively
there are no major distracting inclusions
Cutting is particularly important because the cutter must decide how the natural color zones will appear in the finished stone.

Treatment and Price
Natural unheated bi-color sapphire can receive a premium when the colors and overall quality are attractive. Heat treatment is accepted for sapphire, but laboratory confirmation of no evidence of heat can add rarity and value to fine-quality stones.

Price note: Bi-color sapphire has no fixed price per carat. The US$100–$4,000+ range is only a rough market guide. The exact value depends heavily on the beauty of the color combination, clarity, cut, size, treatment status, certification, rarity and buyer demand.

Spinel Price in Sri Lanka
Spinel is an important natural gemstone found in Sri Lanka and occurs in a wide range of colors, including red, pink, purple, blue, violet and orange. In this Sri Lanka gems price list, natural spinel is estimated at around US$50 to US$2,000+ per carat, while fine red, vivid pink or exceptional blue stones can sell substantially above this range.

GIA notes that the most valuable spinel colors are generally bright red, cobalt blue, vivid pink and vivid orange, while pale lavender material is usually more affordable.

Why Spinel Prices Vary So Much
Unlike sapphire, spinel is typically valued heavily by color intensity and rarity of the color.

Fine red spinel is particularly desirable, followed by exceptional cobalt-blue material, vivid pink and bright orange. Purple, violet and lavender spinels can still be attractive but generally trade at lower levels unless the color is especially strong.

Clarity also matters. GIA notes that spinel with no eye-visible inclusions is preferred, and value generally decreases as inclusions become more noticeable.

Cut quality has a strong influence because properly proportioned spinel can show excellent brilliance. Oval and cushion shapes are especially common, although cutters often choose the final shape according to the rough to preserve as much weight as possible.

Size can also push prices higher. Fine-color spinel becomes increasingly rare above about 5 carats, and GIA notes that prices can rise sharply for fine red, pink and blue stones in larger sizes.

Spinel is generally less commonly treated than sapphire, but for a significant purchase, an independent laboratory report can help confirm whether the stone is natural and identify detectable treatments.

Price note: Spinel has no fixed price per carat. The US$50–$2,000+ range is only rough market guidance. Fine red, vivid pink, cobalt-blue, large or highly transparent stones may sell well above it.

Cobalt Spinel Price in Sri Lanka
Cobalt-blue spinel is one of the rarer and more desirable blue varieties of natural spinel. Sri Lanka is a documented source of natural cobalt-blue spinel, including material from the Ratnapura region.

As a rough market guide, attractive cobalt-blue spinel may range from around US$300 to US$5,000+ per carat, while exceptional vivid-blue, highly transparent or larger stones may sell substantially above this range.

Natural cobalt spinel from Sri Lanka
What Makes Cobalt Spinel Valuable?
For cobalt spinel, the quality of the blue color matters more than simply detecting cobalt. GIA specifically notes that vivid cobalt-blue spinels are highly valued because of their rich saturated blue appearance; the presence of cobalt itself does not automatically make a stone valuable.

The strongest prices are generally associated with stones showing:

vivid, saturated blue color
good brightness rather than excessive darkness
high transparency
few distracting inclusions
good proportions and brilliance
larger size while maintaining fine color
Fine-color spinel becomes increasingly rare above approximately 5 carats, which can substantially affect the value of larger high-quality stones.

Laboratory Testing Is Important
Blue spinel deserves particular care when purchasing because GIA has documented cobalt-diffusion treatment of natural spinel, where treatment introduces blue color into the stone.

For a valuable cobalt-blue spinel, an independent laboratory report can therefore help confirm that the stone is natural and identify detectable treatments. GIA reports can document whether spinel is natural or laboratory-grown and note detectable treatment.

Price note: Cobalt spinel has no fixed price per carat. The US$300–US$5,000+ range is only rough market guidance. Exceptional color, clarity, larger size, untreated status, laboratory documentation and rarity can push individual stones significantly higher.

Star Spinel Price in Sri Lanka
Star spinel shows a star-like optical effect when illuminated by a direct light source. It is much less commonly encountered than ordinary faceted spinel.

Prices may begin around US$30 per carat and rise to approximately US$1,500+ per carat for better examples.

The sharpness, position and movement of the star are particularly important. Body color also influences desirability, with attractive purple, reddish or other unusual colors often attracting more interest than very dark material. Rare high-quality stones can exceed the general price range depending on star sharpness, color, size and collector demand.

Natural green zircon from Sri Lanka

Garnet Price in Sri Lanka
Garnet is a gemstone group rather than one single variety, which is why prices can differ considerably.

In this Sri Lanka gems price list, garnet is estimated at around US$20 to US$800+ per carat. Sri Lankan garnets include attractive red and purplish-red stones such as rhodolite, while the exact value depends strongly on the variety and overall quality.

Natural garnet gemstone from Sri Lanka
The important question when valuing garnet is which garnet variety it is. Color and transparency can matter more than size alone. A lively, well-cut purplish-red rhodolite with strong brilliance may be more desirable than a larger but dark or poorly cut garnet.

Because garnet covers several varieties and quality levels, there is no single fixed garnet price per carat.
